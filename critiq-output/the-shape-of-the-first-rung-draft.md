# The Shape of the First Rung: How AI Changes What Entry-Level Jobs Ask People to Do

*Rough methods manuscript. The title names the research question, not an established causal result.*

## Abstract

Entry-level work can provide practice that later enables checking, planning and supervision. We propose comparing the phases of work emphasized by junior and senior advertisements within occupations, then testing whether the gap is associated with independent measures of reported AI production use. Occupational task inventories, recruitment advertisements and vendor usage indicators describe different constructs. A daily posting panel can track recruiting volume and stated experience, but comparable task-language trends require repeated descriptions with verified coverage. The study is descriptive; no estimates of AI effects, lost training or employment are reported.

## Introduction

An employer can ask a junior to review generated work without advertising the production practice needed to develop that judgment. Conversely, a job posting can emphasize review simply because an employer uses a template or seeks a specialist. We ask whether the difference in advertised production and oversight between junior and senior positions varies with observed vendor-specific production use, and whether any difference persists across comparable recruiting cohorts. A hypothesis of reduced production practice would be weakened by stable or greater junior production emphasis in high-use occupations, but ads alone cannot measure what new hires learn.

This paper's primary object is the **within-occupation rung gap**. [The Junior’s Curve](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/the-juniors-curve) separately examines entry-level STEM employer requirements and changes to developers’ day-to-day work with coding agents. Related publications should disclose overlapping data and distinguish their estimands.

## Methods

The [project overview](../source/aiei-cohort-4-project-overview.md) distinguishes a January 2024 posting snapshot, O*NET 31.0 and a more recent daily employer panel. Treat the 2024 snapshot as a historical cross-section, not the first period of the later panel. Daily panel metadata can support distinct opening counts, level composition and duration when observed, not a textual phase time series. The plan records one description snapshot in November 2026; starting a repeated description series remains a decision. Freeze source versions, first-seen and last-seen rules, included employers, worksite confidence, title matching and duplicates before estimating trends. Separate role seniority, explicit minimum years of experience, and presence of a recognizable level marker. Report unclassified advertisements separately and review a sample of title and duty assignments. Use both broad and strict junior definitions as prespecified sensitivity checks. For any experience estimate, publish the number with stated minima and the unknown share; a missing minimum is not zero years.

Anchor the approach to the six phases of [A New Shape for Work](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/a-new-shape-for-work); report per-level support and do not present a gap when either group is too sparse. Use phase-coded phrase share as a sensitivity measure, not as a substitute for ad share. Compare production and checking first; label planning and coordination comparisons exploratory unless declared in advance. No difference in group fractions is evidence of time spent.

Use the planned Microsoft work-activity measures and Anthropic task penetration source as separate exposures, never a combined score. Preserve assistance versus performance where actually supplied, distinguish vendor samples and scoring differences, and quantify missing joins before modeling. Compare gaps across occupations within occupation families using a transparent regression and show the raw points; use employer-sensitive uncertainty estimates where feasible. ## Results to report

No comparative result is reported. Figure 1 should show the entry and senior production and checking fractions and number of ads in each level, with occupation coverage separate. Figure 2 should show rung gaps against each vendor measure in separate panels and distinguish missing matches from zero use. A third panel should show posting metadata cohorts and verified description cohorts side by side before any phase trend. An eligible metadata record without text is not evidence of no task mention. Examples in the proposal are leads requiring a rerun and denominator check, not headline findings.

## Discussion

The analyses can show an association between recruiting language and vendor-specific AI use. They cannot establish that AI removed learning opportunities, that advertised skills are exercised after hire, or that changing recruitment caused a reduction in employment. A separate, optional diary design could investigate actual practice, contingent on institutional approval and recruitment. The paper should identify which phase differences are robust enough to justify that test. Only publish derived outputs permitted by source terms and with appropriate minimum cell sizes; do not release raw advertisements or participant records.
