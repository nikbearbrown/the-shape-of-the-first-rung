# Help write The Shape of the First Rung

*The Shape of the First Rung* asks whether employers change what they ask entry-level workers to do as AI takes on production work: less producing, the work juniors used to learn from, and more checking, directing and coordinating.

**Where it stands:** there is a research plan and no manuscript, analysis or data in this repo. The plan, [source/aiei-cohort-4-project-overview.md](source/aiei-cohort-4-project-overview.md), was written as a draft grant application that has not been submitted, so its dates are plans. The data it would use lives in the related repos listed in the [README](README.md).

**If you have an hour:** read the research question and the four sub-questions in the [README](README.md) and [open an issue](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/the-shape-of-the-first-rung/issues/new) with one alternative explanation the design should rule out.

## Pick a way to help

| Contribution | Where to start | What to send |
|---|---|---|
| **Review the method** | The methodology section of the [research plan](source/aiei-cohort-4-project-overview.md) | A confound, a comparison that is not fair, or a claim the data could not support. The plan says results are descriptive, not causal; say where the wording slips. |
| **Define the "rung gap"** | Objective 2 of the research plan | The plan measures how entry-level and senior postings differ within an occupation. Propose an exact definition and say what it would get wrong. |
| **Add literature** | The research question | Studies of entry-level hiring, apprenticeship, or how expertise is built on the job. Give the primary source, the population and the years. |
| **Check a number** | The early evidence in the research plan, against [A New Shape for Work](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/a-new-shape-for-work) | Pick one figure. Report the data version, the numerator and denominator, and the value you get. |
| **Name hidden practice** | Sub-question 3 | For a field you know, a routine entry-level task that builds later expertise and that AI now does. State when your example applies. |
| **Sort out the boundary with The Junior's Curve** | [The Junior's Curve](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/the-juniors-curve) | The two projects overlap. Say which questions belong to which, or argue that they should be one paper. |

## Rules for this repo

- **Postings are not work.** Job ads show what employers ask for. They do not show hiring, working time or learning.
- **No raw job ads.** Do not post posting text or per-posting rows. Send counts and derived measures.
- **No worker records.** The plan mentions a possible diary study that would need IRB approval. Nothing of that kind exists yet, and no personal accounts should be added here.

## How to submit

For a discussion, [open an issue](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/the-shape-of-the-first-rung/issues/new) with the file or section, your evidence, and a proposed next step. For an edit, fork, branch, and open a focused pull request. Say what you changed and how you checked it. If you use an AI-generated answer, verify its citations and claims before submitting it.

If you want to collaborate beyond a single contribution, open an issue describing what you'd like to take on, and read [Credit and what we expect from authors](#credit-and-what-we-expect-from-authors) first.

## Credit and what we expect from authors

The author list isn't decided yet. We follow [Yale's guidance on authorship](https://provost.yale.edu/policies/academic-integrity/guidance-authorship-scholarly-or-scientific-publications). Under it, an author has done **all four** of these:

1. **Contributed to a component of the work:** its conception, design, conduct, analysis, or interpretation of at least part of the results.
2. **Helped write the paper:** drafted part of it, or revised it for substance, not just copy edits.
3. **Approved the final version** before submission.
4. **Agreed to be accountable** for the work, including helping answer questions about its accuracy or integrity.

Some contributions are valuable but don't by themselves make someone an author: a single issue, one checked number, or an example from your own field. Everyone who contributes that way is thanked by name in the acknowledgments, unless they ask not to be. To be an author, take on a component, such as building the rung-gap measure, running the cross-section analysis for a family of occupations, or owning the literature review, and then do the writing, approval and accountability steps too. An AI model can't be an author, since it can't approve the paper or answer for it.

If you want to be an author, we expect you to:

- **Read the whole paper,** not just your section. You're putting your name on all of it.
- **Think about it, then give feedback.** Does the method answer the question? Are the comparisons fair? Do the numbers in the text match the tables, figures and data?
- **Suggest changes when they matter.** Don't make a suggestion just to have made one. If you find nothing to change, say so; that's a useful answer. A real problem, raised clearly with a proposed fix, is the most valuable thing you can give.
- **Disclose AI assistance.** AI tools are fine for code and drafting, but say where you used them. You stay accountable for everything you contribute.
- **Keep a record of what you did:** what, when, and where it lives (issue, pull request, commit, figure). The paper's contribution statement is drawn from it and lists each author's [CRediT roles](https://credit.niso.org/).
- **Sign off only when you can stand behind it.** Before submission, every author confirms in writing that they have read the full paper, agree with it, and accept accountability for it. If something is wrong or unclear, say so before approval, not after publication.

Raise authorship early, and we'll revisit it as roles change. Author order follows contribution. If you think your work merits authorship and it isn't reflected, say so before the final draft. Sending an issue or pull request does not by itself settle authorship.
